The gap

The chart records the decisions. The conversation is where the care happens — and today it vanishes at 5 p.m.

Quality, training, and trust all depend on what was actually said: the question nobody asked, the dose that was mumbled, the moment a patient said "yeah, but…" and stopped being honest.

By the time anyone reviews a visit — if anyone does — the only record is what the clinician chose to write down. The patient's side is gone.

OverChart keeps the other side. Every room, every visit, reviewable.

What you get

Visibility that used to require a time machine.

01Full coverage, not sampling

Every conversation in every room becomes searchable the next morning. Monday's review meeting starts from "show me every visit where a dose wasn't confirmed" — not from whichever chart someone happened to pull.

02Truth, with timestamps

When a complaint, a claim, or a question lands — "what actually happened in room 3, Tuesday, around two?" — the answer is on screen in seconds, speaker by speaker.

03Training that compounds

Yesterday's hard visits become tomorrow's teaching cases, indexed and searchable. New clinicians learn from the shape of thousands of real encounters, not one supervisor's memory of her favorite cases.

04Good care, provable

Care that was excellent but poorly documented looks like care that wasn't. A complete transcript defends your clinicians as readily as it audits them — and patients get the honest record too.

Why clinics can actually deploy it

Built for practice, not for the lab.

Quietly small

A palm-sized unit and a discreet microphone per room. No cameras, no server room, no construction. Add rooms at the speed of your budget.

Works through bad days

Recording survives network outages; catch-up happens when the link returns. A slow afternoon is never a lost afternoon of coverage.

Private by construction

Conversations live where your policy says they must — in the clinic, on your network. The data path in and out is small enough to read, and built to be audited.

Made to be read

Transcripts are built for busy reviewers: speakers separated, interruptions marked, a ten-minute visit scannable in under one. It advises nothing; clinicians decide.
