The problem

The chart records the decisions. The conversation is where the care happens — and today it vanishes.

Quality review, training, and liability all depend on what was actually said: the question that wasn't asked, the dose that was mumbled, the moment a patient said "yeah, but…" and stopped being honest.

Manual review can sample maybe one visit in a hundred. OverChart reads all of them — every transcript carries who spoke, when, and over whom.

We built the transcript layer first: capture → diarization → transcription → speaker-labeled transcript → optional review-model scoring. The review layer reads transcripts; it never replaces the clinician.

How it works
01

Capture

One small omnidirectional mic per room. Lossless FLAC, ~1.3 GB per room per day, held locally up to 30 days. No streaming, no always-on network dependency.
02

Separate speakers

Whole-visit diarization. Not "who talked after the last pause" — who spoke when, including interruptions and overlaps, which is where clinical signal actually hides.
03

Transcribe

Word-timestamped transcription tuned for conversation. Clinical vocabulary without keyword hacks — drug names came through unboosted in our own testing.
04

Merge & score

Timestamps and speakers combine into a reviewable transcript. An optional review model scores the visit against a rubric — for quality and training, not for diagnosis.

Deployment

One system. Two shapes.

Same software, one mode flag — chosen per location by wiring, budget, and privacy posture. Not two products.

Profile A — capture & upload

Cheapest possible room box
  • The room box records and ships. Audio leaves nightly; processing happens centrally.
  • Least compute per room — a $200–400 class box suffices.
  • The trade: ~2 GB of audio per clinic per day crosses the network.

Profile B — transcribe in the room

Only text leaves the room
  • The room box transcribes locally. Raw audio never needs to reach a server.
  • Transcripts are thousands of times smaller than the audio they came from — storage and bandwidth shrink to noise.
  • The trade: more capable hardware per room; the same pipeline, slower engines.

The same pipeline, three compute tiers

Portability lives in a swappable inference engine behind fixed interfaces — not in rewriting the system per device.

What this is, honestly

Works today

  • End-to-end pipeline — capture format to speaker-labeled transcript, unattended, with watchdogs and memory ceilings on every step.
  • A measurement harness — known-answer mock consults so every change is tested against truth, not vibes.
  • Hardware freedom — proven on a unified-memory Linux box, designed to run on commodity AMD/Intel with CPU engines.

Not yet — and deliberately

  • No patient audio has been processed. A handling protocol comes before any real recording, full stop.
  • Not a medical device. The review layer grades conversations for quality and training — it does not diagnose or advise treatment.
  • Compliance not yet mapped. Privacy, retention, and HIPAA posture are design inputs being set now, not retrofitted promises.
